About

Hurrix

The tropical cyclones the warning centres are tracking right now, on a globe of live weather-satellite pictures. Nothing on the globe is drawn by hand: every cloud is a pixel a satellite measured, refreshed through the day.

Shows
Each active storm by its official name, with a sharp close-up around it: a 1 km true-colour photo by day, 2 km infrared at night. Cloud heights and 3D storm lift those same pixels to the height their temperature implies.
Imagery
GOES-19, GOES-18 and Himawari-9 from NOAA open data on AWS. Meteosat-12 and Meteosat-9 from EUMETView, © EUMETSAT.
Storms
Names, official winds and past tracks from the National Hurricane Center and the Joint Typhoon Warning Center.
Earth
Where no satellite saw daylight: NASA Blue Marble, with Black Marble city lights at night, via the three-globe examples.
3D
Cloud heights are modelled from cloud-top temperature (about 6.3 °C colder per km), not measured.
